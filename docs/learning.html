Key concepts

The basics

Short explainers on the core ideas behind freedom of religion or belief.

The inner freedom (forum internum)

The freedom to have, adopt, or change a religion or belief is absolute. No one may be coerced into holding or abandoning a belief, and this inner freedom can never be limited — not even in times of public emergency.

Manifesting religion or belief

Worship, observance, practice and teaching — alone or with others, in public or private. This outward expression can only be limited by law, and only when necessary to protect public safety, order, health, morals or the fundamental rights of others.

Non-discrimination

No one should be discriminated against by the State or anyone else because of their religion or belief. Religious minorities have the same rights as majority communities.

Protection of places of worship

Religious buildings and sites are protected under international human rights law and, during armed conflict, under international humanitarian law. Deliberately attacking them, or using them for military purposes, can amount to serious violations.

Freedom from incitement to hatred

Advocacy of religious hatred that constitutes incitement to discrimination, hostility or violence must be prohibited by law — while protecting freedom of expression.

International standards

Where the right comes from

The international instruments and mechanisms that define and protect freedom of religion or belief.

  1. 1948

    Universal Declaration of Human RightsArticle 18

    Everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including the freedom to change their religion or belief, and to manifest it in teaching, practice, worship and observance.

  2. 1966

    International Covenant on Civil and Political RightsArticles 18, 20, 26 & 27

    Protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion, prohibits advocacy of religious hatred that incites discrimination, hostility or violence, and guarantees equality and minority rights.

  3. 1981

    UN Declaration on the Elimination of All Forms of Intolerance and of Discrimination Based on Religion or BeliefUN General Assembly

    Sets out in detail the freedoms included in the right — from establishing places of worship to teaching and observing holidays.

  4. 2012

    Rabat Plan of ActionUN OHCHR

    Guidance on distinguishing protected expression from incitement to hatred.

  5. Today

    UN Special RapporteursMandates

    The UN Special Rapporteur on Freedom of Religion or Belief and the UN Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Myanmar receive information on violations — including from civil society networks like ours.
