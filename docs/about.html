About Myanmar FoRB Network

An independent, inclusive civil society network

The Myanmar Freedom of Religion or Belief Network (Myanmar FoRB Network) is an independent, inclusive civil society network established in 2021 shortly after the February 2021 military coup in Myanmar.

Bringing together advocates from diverse religious and ethnic backgrounds, including faith leaders (religious leaders) of different religions, human rights defenders, civil society actors, researchers, and journalists, Myanmar FoRB Network works to promote and protect the right to freedom of religion or belief for everyone in a situation where the civic space has been extremely shrinking, with high risk for human rights defenders and FoRB advocates.

Who we bring together

What We Do

Human rights work focused on FoRB

Myanmar Freedom of Religion or Belief Network conducts the following human rights work, focusing on FoRB.

(A)

Documentation

Myanmar FoRB Network documents violations of FoRB, particularly the deaths of faith leaders which are directly or implicitly associated with the conflict context in the aftermath of the coup, the abduction, arrest, and prosecution of faith leaders, damage, restrictions, and destruction of places of worship (religious buildings and sites), and the State's illegitimate restriction and prohibition of FoRB practices, and social hostility and harmful hate speech against faith minorities since the February 2021 coup.

(B)

Advocacy

Myanmar FoRB Network advocates for FoRB at the regional level and international level. Our advocacy includes presenting updates on the FoRB situation in Myanmar/Burma, based on its research findings and trend and pattern analysis at regional and international conferences and workshops; engaging with and submitting reports of FoRB violations to the UN Special Rapporteur on the human rights situation in Myanmar and the UN Special Rapporteur on Freedom of Religion or Belief; and coordination and collaboration with national stakeholders and relevant human rights organisations and individuals on FoRB issues in the Myanmar context.

(C)

Awareness Raising

Myanmar FoRB Network conducts FoRB awareness-raising for both the public and other FoRB advocates. The network occasionally organises online webinars for the public on special days, such as International Religious Freedom Day, and regularly organises technical and thematic discussion sessions, such as documenting FoRB violations in an ethical, systematic and survivor-centred approach or differences between blasphemy and dangerous hate speech in accordance with international human rights standards.

    Our Vision

    We envision a nation of Myanmar where the right to freedom of thought, conscience, and religion is fully respected by the State and non-state actors and protected from illegitimate and unjust restrictions, discrimination, hostility, and violence based on religious and religion-affiliated identities in any circumstances, including the event of armed conflicts and war.

    Network developments

    Stefanus Prize and Network Developments

    As a loose network working on sensitive issues of FoRB, Myanmar FoRB Network has passed through more than five years despite high vulnerability, challenges, and limitations escalated in the post-coup period, mainly perpetrated by the Myanmar military. The network has routinely and consistently monitored, shared, discussed, and updated the FoRB situation among network members and occasionally organised knowledge-sharing sessions and skill-building training sessions for the members as well.

    In 2025, Myanmar FoRB Network organised its first Network Strengthening and Strategic Planning Meeting, where network members developed the network ToRs, adopted crucial policies and comprehensively discussed ongoing FoRB and intersectional issues in Myanmar.

While membership in the Myanmar FoRB Network represents a variety of religions, beliefs, ethnicities, geographic areas, and contexts, safety and security for all members of the network is always the network's priority in the midst of escalating state-sponsored discrimination, armed conflicts, and oppression of freedom of religion or belief.
