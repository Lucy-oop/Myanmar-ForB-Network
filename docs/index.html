Freedom of Religion or Belief is a fundamental human right that encompasses the freedom to adopt, practise, manifest, or change one's religion or belief, individually or collectively, in public or private.

FoRB in Myanmar

What is happening in Myanmar regarding FoRB

Myanmar is home to diverse religious, ethnic, and cultural communities, where Buddhism, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, and indigenous spiritual traditions coexist. According to the 2014 national census, 89.8% of the total population practices Buddhism, followed by Christianity with 6.3%, Islam with 2.3%, animism with 0.8%, and Hinduism with 0.5%.

  • Buddhism 89.8%
  • Christianity 6.3%
  • Islam 2.3%
  • Animism 0.8%
  • Hinduism 0.5%

Source: 2014 national census.

Myanmar has often experienced communal violence based on religion or ethnicity since before the 1948 Independence, and the successive governments and military regimes have weaponised Buddhism to take advantage. After precarious democratisation from 2011 to 2020, the Myanmar military, the Tatmadaw as it describes itself, staged the latest coup on 1 February 2021.

  1. 1948Independence. Communal violence based on religion or ethnicity predates it.
  2. 2011–2020A precarious period of democratisation.
  3. 1 Feb 2021Military coup. FoRB violations escalate sharply.
  4. TodayThe Network documents, advocates and raises awareness.

Since 1 February 2021

Religious freedom has deteriorated significantly

Since the coup, the situation of human rights and religious freedom in Myanmar has deteriorated significantly. The expansion of armed conflict, military operations, airstrikes, displacement, and the destruction of civilian infrastructure have had devastating consequences for religious communities.

Airstrikes & military operations

The expansion of armed conflict and the destruction of civilian infrastructure.

Displacement

Communities forced from their homes and cut off from their places of worship.

Places of worship hit

Churches, monasteries, mosques, and other religious buildings have been damaged, destroyed, occupied, or used for military purposes.

Faith leaders targeted

Religious leaders, clergy, and members of faith communities have also been subjected to arrest, violence, intimidation, and killings.

Restrictions and prohibition of legitimate FoRB practices have been increasing, along with the resurrection of state-sponsored ethnocentricism. These violations have occurred alongside broader restrictions on freedom of expression, association, peaceful assembly, and access to information, further limiting the ability of communities to document violations and seek protection.

Our response

A network established after the 2021 military coup

In response to these challenges, the Myanmar Freedom of Religion or Belief (FoRB) Network was established after the 2021 military coup to strengthen cooperation among civil society organisations, human rights defenders, and individuals working to promote and protect religious freedom.

Through documentation, research, advocacy, capacity-building, and collective engagement, Myanmar FoRB Network seeks to raise awareness of FoRB violations, amplify the voices of affected communities, and promote accountability for human rights abuses.

What we do

How we work

Document

violations of FoRB since the February 2021 coup.

We record what happens to faith leaders, places of worship and faith minorities, and analyse the trends and patterns.

  • Deaths of faith leaders
  • Abduction, arrest & prosecution
  • Damage to places of worship
  • State restrictions & hate speech

Advocate

for FoRB at the regional and international level.

We present our findings at conferences and report FoRB violations to UN mechanisms.

  • UN Special Rapporteur on Myanmar
  • UN Special Rapporteur on FoRB
  • Regional & international conferences
  • National stakeholders

Raise awareness

of FoRB among the public and fellow advocates.

We run public webinars on special days and regular technical and thematic sessions.

  • International Religious Freedom Day
  • Survivor-centred documentation
  • Blasphemy vs. dangerous hate speech
  • Knowledge-sharing & training
