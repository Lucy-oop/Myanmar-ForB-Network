FoRB in Myanmar

A right challenged by decades of conflict

The enjoyment of this fundamental right has been increasingly challenged by decades of armed conflict, structural discrimination, religious intolerance, and restrictions on fundamental freedoms.

Religious minorities, particularly in conflict-affected areas, face discrimination, insecurity and restrictions on practising their faith freely.

Many faiths, one country

Home to diverse religious, ethnic and cultural communities

Myanmar is home to diverse religious, ethnic, and cultural communities, where Buddhism, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, and indigenous spiritual traditions coexist.

Myanmar has often experienced communal violence based on religion or ethnicity since before the 1948 Independence, and the successive governments and military regimes have weaponised Buddhism to take advantage. After precarious democratisation from 2011 to 2020, the Myanmar military, the Tatmadaw as it describes itself, staged the latest coup on 1 February 2021.

  • Buddhism 89.8%
  • Christianity 6.3%
  • Islam 2.3%
  • Animism 0.8%
  • Hinduism 0.5%

Source: 2014 national census.

Since 1 February 2021

Religious freedom has deteriorated significantly

The expansion of armed conflict, military operations, airstrikes, displacement, and the destruction of civilian infrastructure have had devastating consequences for religious communities.

Airstrikes & military operations

Expanding armed conflict and the destruction of civilian infrastructure.

Displacement

Communities forced from their homes and cut off from their places of worship.

Places of worship hit

Churches, monasteries and mosques damaged, destroyed, occupied or used for military purposes.

Faith leaders targeted

Religious leaders, clergy and believers subjected to arrest, violence, intimidation and killings.

Restrictions and prohibition of legitimate FoRB practices have been increasing, along with the resurrection of state-sponsored ethnocentricism, further limiting the ability of communities to document violations and seek protection.

Our response

The Myanmar FoRB Network was established after the 2021 military coup.

It strengthens cooperation among civil society organisations, human rights defenders, and individuals working to promote and protect religious freedom.

2021Established shortly after the February 2021 coup
30+Members, both individuals and organisations
5+Years of monitoring FoRB despite high risk

We document

01 — DOCUMENTATION & RESEARCH

Through documentation and research, we raise awareness of FoRB violations since the February 2021 coup.

  • Deaths of faith leaders
  • Abduction, arrest & prosecution of faith leaders
  • Damage and destruction of places of worship
  • State restriction of FoRB practices
  • Social hostility & harmful hate speech
  • Trend and pattern analysis

We advocate

02 — ADVOCACY & ENGAGEMENT

We amplify the voices of affected communities and promote accountability at regional and international levels.

  • UN Special Rapporteur on Myanmar
  • UN Special Rapporteur on FoRB
  • Regional & international conferences
  • Coordination with national stakeholders

We strengthen

03 — CAPACITY-BUILDING

We equip members and partners to document violations in an ethical, systematic and survivor-centred way.

  • Knowledge-sharing sessions
  • Skill-building training for members
  • Raising awareness of FoRB
